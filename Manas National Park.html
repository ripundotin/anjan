A river, a forest, and a border

Manas National Park sits in Assam, India, where the Himalayan foothills of Bhutan meet the Brahmaputra plains. The Manas River runs through it, feeding grassland, wetland and tropical forest in one unbroken landscape.

It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, a Project Tiger reserve and an elephant reserve. In the late twentieth century conflict and poaching took a heavy toll, and since then the park has recovered, which is part of what makes it worth visiting today.

Who lives here

Tap a name to read more.

One-horned rhinoceros

Grassland giants that were lost here and later reintroduced. Look for them near open grass and wallows.

Asian elephant

Herds move through the forest edge and cross the river. Early morning is the best time to see them.

Bengal tiger

The park is a tiger reserve, but tigers are shy. Seeing one is rare, so treat it as a gift.

Golden langur

A rare primate with a cream-gold coat, found in the forests of this region and across the Bhutan border.

Pygmy hog and wild water buffalo

Two grassland and wetland species that depend on healthy, undisturbed habitat. Protecting that habitat protects them.

Birds

Hundreds of bird species have been recorded, including the Bengal florican and great hornbill. Bring binoculars.

Plan a visit

Best time
Roughly November to April, when the weather is dry and wildlife is easiest to see. The park is usually closed in the monsoon.
Getting there
Fly into Guwahati, then travel by road to Barpeta Road, the usual base for the park.
Ways to explore
Jeep safari, elephant-corridor drives and river rafting on the Manas. Book through the park's official entry points.
Visit with care
Keep quiet, keep your distance, carry out all your waste and follow your guide's instructions.

Entry rules, fees and open seasons change. Confirm with Assam Forest Department or Manas National Park before you travel.
